This guide teaches the goal of chess, what every piece does, and how to make moves inside Mascott Chess. You do not need to know any chess words before reading this page.

Learn the pieces How to use the app

1. The goal of chess

Each player has a king. Your goal is to trap the computer's king so it is attacked and has no safe move. That is called checkmate. You do not actually remove the king from the board.

White moves first.

Then players take turns: you move, computer moves, you move, and so on.

One move per turn.

Move one piece on your turn, except for one special king move called castling.

You capture enemies.

If your piece moves to a square with a computer piece, that computer piece is removed.

2. Board basics

The chess board has 64 squares: 8 across and 8 down. Light and dark squares alternate. In Mascott Chess, your pieces are always shown at the bottom of the board.

  • Files are the letters a to h across the board.
  • Ranks are the numbers 1 to 8 up the board.
  • A square name combines both, such as e4 or a1.
  • Pieces cannot usually jump over other pieces. The knight is the important exception.

3. How to move in Mascott Chess

1Tap your piece

The app highlights every legal square that piece can move to.

2Choose a highlighted square

A dot means an empty square. A ring means a capture.

3Wait for the computer

The computer thinks and makes a legal reply based on the Elo level.

4. How every piece moves

In the diagrams below, the white piece is in the center area. Blue dots show normal moves. Rings show capture directions. Real games may show fewer moves if another piece blocks the path.

♙

Pawn

Pawns are small but important. A white pawn moves forward toward the top of your screen. A black pawn moves the opposite way.

  • Moves forward one square if the square is empty.
  • From its starting square, it may move two squares forward if both squares are empty.
  • Captures one square diagonally forward, not straight ahead.
  • If a pawn reaches the far end, it promotes into a queen, rook, bishop, or knight.

Rook

The rook moves in straight lines.

  • Moves any number of squares left, right, up, or down.
  • Cannot move diagonally.
  • Cannot jump over pieces.

Bishop

The bishop moves diagonally.

  • Moves any number of squares diagonally.
  • A bishop always stays on the same color square it started on.
  • Cannot jump over pieces.

Queen

The queen is the most powerful piece because it combines rook and bishop movement.

  • Moves any number of squares in a straight line or diagonal.
  • Cannot move like a knight.
  • Cannot jump over pieces.

Knight

The knight has the strangest move, but it is easy to remember as an L-shape.

  • Moves two squares in one direction, then one square sideways.
  • Or one square in one direction, then two squares sideways.
  • The knight can jump over other pieces.

King

Your king is the piece you must protect.

  • Moves one square in any direction.
  • Cannot move onto a square attacked by the computer.
  • If your king is attacked, you are in check and must fix it immediately.

5

Check

Check means your king is under attack. In Mascott Chess, a checked king is highlighted red. On your next move, you must make the king safe.

  • Move the king away.
  • Capture the attacking piece.
  • Block the attack with another piece, if possible.
6

Checkmate

Checkmate means the king is in check and there is no legal way to escape. The game ends immediately.

Beginner idea: before every move, ask “Is my king safe after this?”

7

Stalemate

Stalemate is a draw. It happens when the player to move is not in check but has no legal move.

This often happens when one side has only a king left.

8

Special moves

  • Promotion: a pawn reaching the far end becomes a stronger piece.
  • Castling: a king-and-rook safety move. The app shows it only when legal.
  • En passant: a rare pawn capture. The app shows it only right after it becomes legal.

9. A simple first-game plan

Control the center

Try moving a center pawn first, like the pawn in front of your king or queen.

Bring pieces out

Move knights and bishops toward the center before moving the same piece many times.

Protect your king

If the app says you are in check, look only for moves that make your king safe.

10. What computer Elo should a beginner choose?

Start low. The goal is to learn what the pieces do, not to beat a strong engine immediately.
